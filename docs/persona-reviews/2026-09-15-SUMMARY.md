## How this round was run, and what it therefore cannot tell you

The console was walked **once**, in a single signed-in `developer_admin` session
(`mike@digitalferrari.com`, org scope `*`). Every screen was captured as a screenshot
plus its rendered text, and each persona's review was written against the subset of
screens its own role reaches — 30 for `developer_admin`, 23 for `editor`, 13 for
`support`, 9 for `analyst`, per the `ROLES` matrix in `apps/admin-web/src/rbac.ts`.

**No lower-privileged credential was ever presented.** There is no in-app persona or
role switcher — commit `75d8b5a` added fixtures, a drift test and docs, not a UI — so
embodying seven staff grants would have meant seven real Cognito users. Accordingly,
**every review's "Blocked where I should not have been" and "Reached something I should
not have" sections are filled with an explicit not-exercised line, in all ten files.**
Nothing in this round is a privilege finding. PERSONAS.md calls those two sections the
reason the panel is worth running; they are the one thing this round did not produce.

Also not exercised, and stated in the files that depend on it:

- **Nothing past a submit button.** This stack sends real mail. No send was scheduled,
  no invite issued, no key created, no address suppressed, nothing erased or exported.
- **Both cross-org personas are untestable here.** The deployment has exactly one
  organization, so Rafael's cross-tenant refusal and Tom's branding-bleed check cannot
  run. Their fixture scopes (`summit`, `summit,vail`) do not include the reviewed org.
- **subscriber-web has no URL in the stack outputs**, so Neve and Owen could only
  review the public-web entry points to their journeys, not the journeys.
- **No inbound mail**: double opt-in, magic link and the RFC 8058 one-click
  unsubscribe header were all out of reach.

## Fully evidenced findings

These depend on no RBAC assumption and no unsubmitted flow. Each is a screen the panel
actually saw.

1. **A read-only analyst has no export anywhere.** Marcus's walk ends in "export," and
   no export control renders on any of the nine screens `reports:view` reaches.
   Data & exports is gated on `subscribers:manage`, which `analyst` does not hold — and
   it is a subscriber dump, not report data. The real workflow is retyping percentages.
2. **The authoring loop has no preview.** Priya's walk is compose → build template →
   *preview* → schedule. Compose goes Body mode → When → Schedule. She would press
   Schedule on an MJML body she has never seen rendered.
3. **The `*`-scoped analyst has nothing to aggregate.** Wen's first step is "compare
   performance across orgs." Every screen she can reach is pinned to one org — Usage &
   cost is literally titled `· identithing-newsletter`. Within a single org it is no
   better: Campaigns states outright there is "no bulk rates route," so ranking
   campaigns means opening each report by hand.
4. **Dana's own walk has no completable middle.** Setup declares "3/3 required complete
   — ready to send" while `www.identithing.com` has no SES identity, and there is no
   console action anywhere to create or verify a sending identity. Organizations is a
   bare create form: no list, no row for the current org, despite a plural nav label.
5. **A live org is wearing fixture branding.** The public site serves a **SUMMIT DAILY**
   wordmark over its only newsletter, "Notes from Identithing." `summitdaily` is the
   fixture publisher throughout `packages/core` and the domain tests — this reads as
   demo branding shipped onto a live tenant. Branding's preview is two words
   ("Primary heading" / "Secondary accent") for a page built from masthead, nav,
   eyebrow, headline, card, input and button.
6. **Support has no bounce diagnosis.** Aisha's walk includes "look up why a message
   bounced." `Last engaged` is "—" on every row, and both Suppression and
   Deliverability are hidden from `support`. There is no tool for that step.
7. **The departing subscriber has no self-serve exit.** "You can unsubscribe at any
   time" appears in the footer of every public page, but the only route offered is
   `/preferences`, which asks for an email address and sends *another* email. For
   someone who wants out, that is the opposite of one click. Export and erasure live in
   the admin console, so he cannot self-serve either.
8. **Every public entry point pays off in an email, and the captured configuration
   cannot be shown to deliver any of the three.** Signup relies on a double opt-in mail,
   but Setup reports the account is in the **SES sandbox** — "a valid subscriber address
   will be refused, and the refusal reaches the public signup page." `/preferences`
   offers "Email me a link" for an org whose Identity & pools screen reports magic-link
   signing **off** (no signing key, no linked pool, empty JWKS). And the footer's
   "unsubscribe at any time" has no entry point at all. Whether each one errors, falls
   back, or fails silently is not observable without submitting — which this round did
   not do. This is the sharpest cross-cutting result from the end-user panel.
9. **Two screens load slowly with no skeleton.** Usage & cost rendered only "Loading…"
   on first visit and needed a retry; Audit log sat on "Reading the log…" for several
   seconds. Both resolve.
10. **The audit trail is unreadable without a lookup.** Audit log identifies actors by
    raw Cognito sub (`941804a8-…`) while Roles & access lists members by email — on the
    one screen where identifying the actor is the entire point.
11. **Headline rates have no denominator on the tile.** "50.0% average open rate" is one
    open of two sends. Every trend slot next to it renders as a bare "—".

## Verify next — observations that need a real credential

Both come from an admin render. Neither is a confirmed privilege finding, and neither
is written as one in the persona files.

- **Campaigns renders [Pause] and [Archive] apparently enabled on already-sent
  one-offs**, on a screen gated by `reports:view` — which `analyst` holds. PERSONAS.md
  sent Marcus looking for exactly this shape of false negative. Whether those controls
  are hidden or disabled for `analyst` is the single highest-value thing to check.
- **Data & exports is gated on `subscribers:manage`, which `support` holds, and the
  Erase control renders on it** in the same primary styling as every other button. The
  screen's own copy says "Erase requires the subscribers:delete role and will 403
  otherwise." That is the exact capability line distinguishing Aisha from an editor,
  and it appears to be enforced at the API only.

Related, and cheap to check: the same "Sending identity · live from SES" block (sandbox
status, quota, per-domain DKIM) renders on **Setup, which gates on nothing**, while
Settings' copy says that data is gated on `identity:manage`. And **API & webhooks gates
on no capability at all** yet contains a Create-key form and a live key-validity oracle.

## Two things worth fixing outside the UI

- **A real deliverable personal address is in the live subscriber base.** PERSONAS.md's
  "Keeping this honest" rule requires `@example.com` fixtures precisely because this
  stack sends. A real Gmail address is present as an active subscriber and appears as
  the target of a `privacy.export` in the audit log.
- **Which app is CloudFront serving?** The live public site's strings match
  `apps/subscriber-web/src/App.tsx`, not `apps/public-web/src/App.tsx`. Unconfirmed
  against the deployed bundle, but worth resolving.
