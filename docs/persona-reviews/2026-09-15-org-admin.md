## What worked

The org I am acting on is never ambiguous, and that is the thing I most need. The sidebar's
top block reads **"Identithing Newsletter / newsletter.identithing…"** with a swap glyph
beside it, on every screen; the topbar repeats it as a breadcrumb ("Identithing Newsletter /
Dashboard"); and a green **PROD** badge sits next to it. Several screens then put the org id
in their own heading — *Setup · identithing-newsletter*, *Identity & pools ·
identithing-newsletter*, *Merge tags · identithing-newsletter*, *Usage & cost ·
identithing-newsletter*. I never had to guess whose subscribers I was looking at.

**Roles & access** says the right thing in the right place. "Roles are enforced server-side"
is the sentence a one-tenant admin wants to read before he trusts a hidden nav item, and
"`*` is reserved for the bootstrap administrator and cannot be granted here" tells me the
one grant I am not allowed to hand out and why, at the moment I would otherwise type it into
the Organizations field. That field being free text ("Organizations (comma separated)") next
to that sentence is a good pairing — the constraint is stated where the mistake happens.

**Identity & pools** is honest about what it is not. "Written at provisioning time and mostly
read-only here — the only update is deliberate magic-link signing-key rotation" set my
expectations before I went looking for edit controls that do not exist, and "Magic links are
off for this organization, which is a complete and valid configuration — not a missing step"
stopped me filing a setup gap that is not one.

The **Audit log** framing is exactly right for an admin who may one day be asked to prove
something: "written once to an Object-Locked bucket. Entries cannot be edited or deleted —
including by whoever wrote them." That last clause is what makes the log worth anything, and
it is stated rather than implied.

**Setup** and the Sending identity block are unusually candid — the SES-sandbox warning spells
out that a real subscriber address will be refused *and that the refusal reaches the public
signup page*, and the `www.identithing.com` row says plainly that publishing DNS will not
help because the identity itself is missing. That is a diagnosis, not a status light.

## What confused me

**The screen that is supposed to be about my org is partly about the whole deployment, and
says so in small print.** Settings → Verification & quota states that the live SES route "is
gated on `identity:manage` — the account sandbox and quota describe the whole deployment
rather than this organization." So a screen headed "Deployment configuration for this
organization" shows me a 200-message quota and a sandbox state that are not mine — they are
everyone's. On a one-org deployment I cannot tell the difference and would happily read
"6 sent in the last 24 hours" as my org's number. Add a second tenant and that reading is
simply wrong, and nothing on the screen visually separates the deployment-wide rows from the
org-scoped ones. This is the finding I would most want checked before a second org exists.

**"Organizations" is a create form with no list.** The nav item is plural and sits under
CONFIGURE; the screen is titled "Add organization" and contains only fields — Name, Sending
domain, Site domain, Time zone, Environment, Magic-link tokens, and a Create button. There is
no roster of existing organizations, not even the one I am in. So the only thing this screen
lets a one-tenant admin do is **manufacture a second tenant**, and it never says whether the
org I create lands in my scope, in nobody's, or in the bootstrap administrator's. "Provisioning
is idempotent on the derived org id" tells me about retries; it tells me nothing about who
owns the result. For someone whose whole job is one tenant, the plural label promises a
management surface and delivers a factory.

**The org switcher is an affordance with nothing behind it that I can see.** The sidebar block
carries a swap glyph, which reads as "there are others." Here there is exactly one org, so I
have no way to know what that control does — whether it opens a picker of the orgs on my claim,
whether it would be inert for me, or whether it is the very surface where cross-tenant refusal
would be visible. That is precisely the thing this deployment cannot show me.

**The Audit log's Scope selector offers me "Cross-org" and never says what that means for
someone who holds one org.** The options are `identithing-newsletter` and
`Cross-org (org creation, pool linking)`. If I select Cross-org, do I get the cross-org events
of *my* org, an empty list, or a refusal? The parenthetical names the event types but not the
visibility rule, and with one org in the system every possible answer produces the same
single-row result. Alongside that: the "Who" column shows a raw Cognito sub
(`941804a8-9061-…`) while Roles & access lists the same people by email. I cannot connect a
privileged action to a teammate without a lookup the console does not offer — and the audit
log is the one screen where knowing *who* is the entire point.

**Identity & pools mixes stack-level and org-level in one list.** The heading is
`· identithing-newsletter`, and the first card is "Admin user pool · staff" tagged
`[shared across orgs]` with a pool ID, app client ID and hosted UI domain. The copy does say
"It is stack-level, not part of any organization" — but it is rendered above my org's own
subscriber-pool card in the same visual stack, so the tag is doing all the work of a boundary
that the layout does not draw.

**Roles & access has no example of a scoped member.** The only row is
`mike@digitalferrari.com … "14 capabilities" … all organizations`. The header promises "the
organizations listed here scope what each member can act on," but this deployment contains no
member for whom that column says anything other than *all*. The scoping column — the thing I
am here to verify — is untested UI.

**Usage & cost took two visits.** The first rendered only the heading and "Loading…" for over
two seconds; the second needed a six-second wait before it resolved to a period table. The
Audit log did the same thing ("Reading the log…" for ~3s). Neither screen tells me whether a
long wait means a slow query or an empty result, which matters more on a screen where an
empty result could also mean *scoped away from you*.

**Cost estimator models a multi-tenancy this deployment does not have.** It asks for an
"Organizations" count at "$1/mo KMS key each" and its Fixed line reads "1 stack data key +
1 org key." It is the one screen that treats multiple orgs as normal, and it is the one screen
where that assumption costs nothing to be wrong about.
